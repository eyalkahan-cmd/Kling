# Reference film analysis: the user's finished growth film (girl, 27 photos, 2:57)

This is the film the user made earlier (with Gemini prompts) and likes. The prompts were lost, so the notes below are reverse-engineered from the final video. Use it as the target style. Never copy content.

## Pacing
- 27 photos in 177 s, about 6.5 s per photo including the hold. Each transition is about 4–5 s of motion and 1.5–2 s of calm hold.
- The camera almost never stops between photos. Motion leads straight into the hold, and from the hold into the next motion.

## Transition devices that carried the film (in order of frequency)
1. **Walk away from the camera into the next place, then turn around.** The person walks away, seen from behind or in 3/4 rear view, and the camera follows behind at a medium distance. The new place forms ahead of them. At the destination they turn to face the camera in the N+1 pose. Clothes, hair and accessories change while the back is to the camera, so the swap is barely visible. This is the single most used device, and it is why outfit changes read as smooth there.
2. **Handover chain between people.** The baby is passed from person to person. The receiver is already standing in the next place, and the giver walks out of frame. Example: mother on the sofa → grandmother → grandmother walks out through the window into the garden → grandfather and another grandmother are already waiting there.
3. **Portal through a real opening.** She walks out through a window or glass door into the garden, the frame passes, and the outside is beyond it. Another example: she kneels in front of a big flag and dives into it, it becomes water, she swims underwater and comes up in a pool.
4. **Vehicle carries the scene.** An alpine coaster car rolls right on into a restaurant interior. A stroller exit: the toddler climbs out of the front of the stroller and walks off.
5. **Group stands in place while the world changes behind them.** Kids walk toward the camera on a boardwalk, stop, and the boardwalk turns into a living room behind them. Backpacks grow in at their feet and shirts change. This reads as magic and was accepted, but only with people standing still and calm.
6. **Run or walk sideways with a side reveal.** A hallway wall slides away to reveal the sea on one side. This was accepted in that film because the camera moved fast and the change took about 1 s.

## Environment continuity tricks seen
- Shared lines continue between places: parking-lot white lines become wooden floor plank lines, a boardwalk railing becomes a house window frame, and a sea railing stays a railing across two locations.
- Ground into ground, kept under the feet the whole time.
- Fast follow-camera motion blur is used deliberately for 0.3–0.6 s at the moment of change, but never on the face when it is toward the camera.

## Applying it to our project
- Prefer device 1 (walk away, back to camera, turn at the destination) whenever the person must change clothes, hats or hair. It hides the swap that Kling does in a few frames anyway.
- Use the handover chain when there is an adult in both photos.
- Keep each clip 5–7 s.

## VFX layer seen (general knowledge, not for the current project)
These were used as moments inside or on top of finished clips:
- Opening: from space down to Earth, through clouds, onto a map, into the first room.
- A small plane flying across the sky with a banner reading "happy birthday" in Hebrew.
- A whale breaching in the sea behind a railing.
- A lion behind a fence, and a mammoth in snow behind a railing.
- A dinosaur forest background with flying reptiles.
- A hand covering the lens as a wipe into the next scene. The user later preferred no wipes for normal transitions.
- A pop-art comic background with emoji stickers behind a family selfie.
- A glowing neon heart sculpture at night.
- Ending: an aerial shot over a river, into the clouds, to Earth, and a futuristic city where the girl spins Earth on her finger.

Pattern for all of these: the element sits in the background or the sky, behind the people. People stay locked and unobstructed. The element moves with real physics and is scaled to the scene.
